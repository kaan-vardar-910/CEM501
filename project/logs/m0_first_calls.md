## Prompt 3: Delay Notice (AIA A201)
**Summarize the key requirements for a 'Notice of Delay' according to AIA A201-2017 General Conditions. What are the specific time limits for submittal?**

### Output:
According to the **AIA Document A201-2017 (General Conditions of the Contract for Construction)**, a "Notice of Delay" is governed primarily under the rules for "Claims." Here are the key requirements and specific time limits:

#### 1. Specific Time Limits (The 21-Day Rule)
Under **Section 15.1.3.1 (Notice of Claims)**, claims by either the Owner or Contractor must be initiated by written notice to the other party and to the Initial Decision Maker (typically the Architect). 
*   **Time Limit:** Notice must be given within **21 days** after the occurrence of the event giving rise to the claim, or within **21 days** after the claimant first recognizes the condition giving rise to the claim, whichever is later. 
*   *Failure to provide notice within this 21-day window can result in the waiver of the right to claim a time extension or additional costs.*

#### 2. Substantiation of the Claim
Under **Section 15.1.5 (Claims for Additional Time)**, if the Contractor wishes to make a Claim for an increase in the Contract Time, they must provide:
*   Written notice as required by Section 15.1.3.
*   The claim must include an estimate of the cost and of the probable effect of the delay on the progress of the Work (i.e., how it impacts the critical path).

#### 3. Continuing Contract Performance
Under **Section 15.1.4**, pending final resolution of a Claim (except as otherwise agreed in writing), the Contractor must proceed diligently with performance of the Contract, and the Owner must continue to make payments in accordance with the Contract Documents.

#### 4. Adverse Weather Conditions
Under **Section 15.1.6.2**, if adverse weather conditions are the basis for a Claim for additional time, the Notice must be documented by data substantiating that weather conditions were abnormal for the period of time, could not have been reasonably anticipated, and had an adverse effect on the scheduled construction.
